Curriculum Vitae

Hannah Arendt
Download PDF

Education

1928

Dr. phil. in Philosophy
University of Heidelberg
Advisor: Prof. Karl Jaspers. Dissertation: Der Liebesbegriff bei Augustin (The Concept of Love in Augustine).

1926 – 1928

Graduate study in philosophy
Freiburg & Heidelberg
Phenomenology under Edmund Husserl at Freiburg; philosophy under Karl Jaspers at Heidelberg.

1924 – 1926

Study of philosophy and theology
University of Marburg
Philosophy under Martin Heidegger; New Testament theology under Rudolf Bultmann.

Academic Appointments

1967 – 1975

University Professor
The New School for Social Research, New York
Graduate Faculty of Political and Social Science.

1963 – 1967

Professor
Committee on Social Thought, University of Chicago

1959

Visiting Professor
Princeton University
First woman appointed to a full professorship at Princeton.

1955 – 1960s

Visiting appointments
Berkeley, Columbia, Northwestern, Wesleyan
Including a Fellowship at the Center for Advanced Study, Wesleyan University.

Public & Editorial Work

1961

Correspondent
The New Yorker
Covered the trial of Adolf Eichmann in Jerusalem.

1949 – 1952

Executive Director
Jewish Cultural Reconstruction, New York
Recovering and redistributing Jewish cultural property after the war.

1946 – 1948

Chief Editor
Schocken Books, New York

1935 – 1939

Director of youth work
Youth Aliyah, Paris
Arranged the emigration of Jewish children from Europe to Palestine.

Selected Honours

1975

Sonning Prize for Contributions to European Civilization, University of Copenhagen.

1969

Emerson-Thoreau Medal, American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

1967

Sigmund Freud Prize, German Academy for Language and Literature.

1959

Lessing Prize of the Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg.

1952

Guggenheim Fellowship.

Lectures

1973 – 1974

Gifford Lectures, University of Aberdeen — The Life of the Mind.

1955

Christian Gauss Seminars in Criticism, Princeton University.

Biographical

1906

Born 14 October in Linden, Hanover; raised in Königsberg, East Prussia.

1933

Detained by the Gestapo for research on antisemitism; fled Germany for Paris.

1937 – 1951

Stateless following the revocation of German citizenship; naturalized as a U.S. citizen in 1951.

1941

Emigrated to New York with Heinrich Blücher via Lisbon.
